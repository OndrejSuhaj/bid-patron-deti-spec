## 1. System Purpose

Patronus is a **donation / child-patronage platform** operating across three countries — **CZ / RO / MD**
(Czech Republic, Romania, Moldova). Its reason to exist is to connect a **fundraiser** (who applies for help on
behalf of a **child**) with a **patron** / donor (who funds that help), take the case through a risk gate and a
legal contract, publish it as a public fundraising **Story (Příběh)**, and collect and reconcile the money that
funds it [DOMAIN-kernel §1; EN0001; EN0004; EN0008].

The domain revolves around three primary concepts and the money that moves through them:

- **Lead / Application (Žádost)** — the case record. "Lead" is **not** a separate entity; it is the early
  intake/coordination phase of the *same* Application record, so one workflow of ~66 states spans both lead-era
  and application-era states on a single field [EN0001; DOMAIN-kernel §1; INV19]. Handled as the case-record
  aggregate (EN0001, AG1) — served by the Application-Lifecycle, Application-Status-Orchestrator and
  ApplicationAction-Processor SRVs (C1); its status fan-out reads ApplicationReaction (EN0026), appends
  ApplicationLog (EN0025), and drives Scoring-&-Risk / Blacklist (C2, EN0016/EN0017) and the Facebook
  lead-intake surface [modules.md §2] — and use-cases UC0001 (submit), UC0002 (orchestrate status change),
  UC0016 (dedup/merge).
- **Story / Campaign (Příběh)** — the public fundraising story generated from an approved Application; carries a
  target amount, a running raised total, a deadline and a lifecycle status [EN0004]. Handled as the Campaign/Story
  aggregate (EN0004, AG2) and UC0011 (campaign/story lifecycle).
- **Donation / Transaction** — every incoming payment (one-off, corporate, recurring, voucher, or bank/AISP
  import) [EN0009]. Handled as the Transaction aggregate (EN0009, AG3) and the RecurringTransaction aggregate
  (EN0010, AG4), captured through the per-region gateway adapters (ComGate/Netopia/MAIB, C4), and
  UC0005 (donate) / UC0006 (gateway callback) / UC0007 (recurring) / UC0008 (reconcile).

Supporting purposes evidenced in the module inventory and UCs: risk scoring and blacklisting (UC0003), contract
generation and e-signature (UC0004), tax documents — CZ donation confirmation and RO 2% redirect (UC0010),
party/CRM management (UC0016), authentication and GDPR (UC0014/UC0015), reporting and CSV export (UC0017), and a
set of integration/infrastructure capabilities (search indexing, ops alerting, workflow engine, invoice import).
Patronus is technically a **custom Drupal (PHP) application** — 52 custom modules, 3 custom themes, exported
config — with Drupal core/contrib out of scope [modules.md §1, §4].

## 2. System Boundaries

**Inside the system (owned by Patronus).** The full Application/Lead → Scoring → Contract → Campaign → Donation
lifecycle and its data: the ~37 custom entities on MariaDB (see §6), the ~66-state Application workflow, the
campaign funding logic, transaction/recurring/voucher handling, reconciliation bookkeeping, document generation,
party records, reporting projections, and the transactional-messaging orchestration [db-inventory §2; DOMAIN-kernel §1;
SRV-target-list Layer D]. All 11 bounded contexts C1..C11 (see §4) live inside the system boundary.

**Outside the system (not owned).** Patronus depends on, but does not own, a set of external systems (see §5):
payment gateways (**ComGate**, **Netopia/MobilPay**, **MAIB**), the **Moneta** bank API and the client **IMAP**
bank-notification inbox, the **Mautic** CRM / marketing platform, **Facebook** (Pixel + Conversions API) and
**Google Tag Manager** analytics, government registries (**MVČR** invalid-document check, **ARES** business
registry), the **Nager.Date** public-holiday calendar, **OneDrive / Microsoft Graph** invoice storage,
**Elasticsearch** (audit-log store + search index + a separate Elastic Cloud `organisations` index), and the
**Slack** / **Telegram** ops-alerting channels [integrations.md §1–§10; SRV-architecture-map §4].

**Party model (who acts inside the boundary).** Every actor is a **User** differentiated by *role*, not by a
separate account type [EN0008; db-inventory §5 lists 15 roles incl. administrator, coordinator,
senior_coordinator, accountant, content_admin, risk_manager, marketing, fundraiser, patron, supporter,
organisation_worker, front, manager]. Personal/institutional detail for any party (child, fundraiser, patron,
school, employer, lead) is held in a single overloaded **Contact** store disambiguated only by a role
discriminator [EN0006; HS08]. Three business words are deliberately overloaded and must be held apart: "Lead" =
intake phase of an Application (above); "Patron" spans a patron *User role*, a patron *Contact*, and a public
*Patron display profile* on a Story (EN0008 / EN0006 / EN0005); "Account" (EN0007) is neither a bank account nor
the Drupal user and is part of a **dormant** recommendation subsystem [DOMAIN-kernel §1; INV27].

**CZ / RO / MD multi-tenant boundary (weak / implicit).** The three countries are served by **one Drupal
instance** with **URL path-prefix language negotiation** (prefixes `cs, en, ru, ro`) and per-country frontends
for CZ and RO (MD has no dedicated frontend) — there is **no domain-based multitenancy** and **no
tenant/country column** on the core entities [integrations.md §10; modules.md §4]. Country behaviour is instead
expressed through runtime country branches, per-region payment modules, and hardcoded country checks — a
structural risk recorded as **HS11** and revisited in §8.

**What the system does NOT own / explicitly excludes.** External registries and gateways above; production DB
credentials / DSN / topology (source is GDPR-scrubbed — `Blocked`) [db-inventory §1, §6]; Drupal core/contrib and
vendored libraries (out of analysis scope) [modules.md]; and the notification *content* matrix (status → role →
text → channel), which is owned by the test-scenarios evidence and the MSG layer, not by this ARCH pass
[integrations.md §2].

## 3. High-Level Architecture

Patronus is a **Drupal 10 (PHP) monolith** built almost entirely from custom modules on top of the Drupal
Entity/Field API, with MariaDB as the primary store [db-inventory §1; modules.md §1]. At a conceptual level it is
layered as follows (no code detail — each layer is a role, evidenced by the cited repo-map surface):

1. **Entry surfaces (web + background).** A single HTTP front controller bootstraps all web requests; ~28 modules
   declare routes (Application/Lead workflow admin, gateway callbacks, a JSON/session API) served by ~56
   controllers and ~185 forms [entrypoints.md §1–§3, §7]. In parallel, background surfaces exist: scheduled
   (cron) work in 9 modules, 5 queue workers, and CLI/console commands [entrypoints.md §8].
2. **Domain entities.** ~37 custom content/config entities carry the bulk of the domain state as code-defined
   base fields (see §6) [db-inventory §2].
3. **Services / orchestration.** ~38 service-definition files across ~28 modules expose domain and workflow
   services (e.g. a workflow service, an action service, mailing, tokens, scheduled publish) [entrypoints.md §5].
   The platform's **main orchestration seam is a custom application-status-change event** consumed by three
   subscribers (notification, reaction, scoring) — the recurring "react to Application status change" pattern
   [entrypoints.md §6; SRV0002; UC0002; HS01].
4. **Integration adapters.** Per-vendor boundaries (payment gateways, bank APIs, CRM, registries, storage, index,
   alerting) live in dedicated modules that wrap an external protocol/SDK (see §5) [integrations.md; SRV-architecture-map §4].
5. **Async processors.** Cron-driven and queue-driven work (recurring charges, reconciliation, CSV export, search
   indexing, scheduled publish, the dormant recommendation processor) run outside the request path
   [entrypoints.md §8; SRV-target-list Layer P].

The SRV reconstruction organises every service into a **4-layer taxonomy** [SRV-architecture-map §2;
SRV-target-list], which is the conceptual architecture a rewrite should preserve:

|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Data Architecture

**Engine & data API.** Patronus persists to **MariaDB** (MySQL-compatible) via the **Drupal 10 Entity/Field API**
(content/config entities with SQL content-entity storage); there is **no Doctrine or other ORM** [db-inventory §1].
Production DSN/credentials/topology are **absent** (GDPR-scrubbed) — the engine is derived from the container
image + enabled driver, not from a live connection (`Blocked`) [db-inventory §1, §6].

**Custom entities (the real data model).** **37 custom entity types** (36 content entities + 1 config entity),
of which **32 are promoted to EN domain entities** and placed into aggregates (13 roots, 10
members, 4 reference/config, 3 content, 2 read-model) [db-inventory §2; DOMAIN-aggregates §4]. The heavyweight
records are the Application and its ~100-field ApplicationProfile (held twice per Application), the Campaign, the
Transaction, and the universal Contact [DOMAIN-kernel §1; HS10, HS08]. A handful of Drupal core entities
(node bundles, user, taxonomy-term, media) carry config fields but are not custom entity types
[db-inventory §2].

**Structural fact — soft references, NO database foreign keys.** Every relation between entities is a **soft
reference (target-id only)**; Patronus enforces **no DB foreign keys** and, on most identity fields,
**no uniqueness constraints** [db-inventory §2, §4; DOMAIN-aggregates modelling caveat; INV23/24/25; HS08]. Almost
all custom tables have **no source-declared schema** — the SQL schema is framework-generated from field
definitions (treated as convention, not evidence); only two custom source-declared tables exist, plus a few
raw-SQL audit/cache tables created via update hooks (e.g. the application status-change audit log, the
email-domain cache) [db-inventory §1, §4]. This absence of RI is the single biggest structural characteristic of the data model and
the root cause of the destructive manual dedup/merge processes [HS08, HS09; INV17/18/19/23].

**Aggregates.** The transactional core is organised into **13 aggregates (AG1..AG13)** — nine that own the
transactional core plus four write-once record/import-audit aggregates — reconstructed as **logical/domain boundaries
(not schema-enforced)** because there are no FKs [DOMAIN-aggregates §1–§2, modelling caveat]. Roots: Application
(AG1), Campaign (AG2), Transaction (AG3), RecurringTransaction (AG4), Contract (AG5), Voucher (AG6), Party/User
(AG7), Organisation (AG8), Risk/Scoring (AG9), DonationConfirmation (AG10), TaxPayer (AG11), BankTransactionMail
(AG12), ComgateBankReconciliation (AG13).

**Read-models / projection entities.** Reporting is served by projection entities — CostsSnapshot (EN0031) and
ReportSnapshot (EN0032) — plus the EmailArchive per-send log (EN0022); these own no transactional lifecycle
[DOMAIN-aggregates §3; UC0017].

**Status / workflow model.** The canonical current status vocabulary is a Drupal content-moderation workflow of
**~66 states** (~40 transitions) bound to the custom Application entity, spanning Lead/Application/Story states on
one field (e.g. `new`, `to_check`, `scoring`/`scoring_ok`, `contract`/`waiting_signature`/`contract_signed`,
`active`, `completed`, `campaign_uncompleted`, the `canceled_*` family, `duplicate`) [db-inventory §5; EN0001;
DOMAIN-kernel §3.1]. Transaction status is a separate enum (PENDING/AUTHORIZED/PAID/CANCELLED/REFUNDED)
[db-inventory §5; DOMAIN-kernel §3.3]. Plus 24 status-group/funnel-filter config entities and 15 user roles
[db-inventory §5].

**Multi-tenant data scoping.** There is **no country/tenant column** on the core entities; CZ/RO/MD data coexists
in the same tables and is separated only by runtime country branches and per-country modules — so cross-tenant
data scoping is not enforced at the data layer (the reason CSV exports are global across countries) [HS07, HS11;
integrations.md §10].

## 7. Operational Model

The runtime surfaces, from [entrypoints.md] cross-referenced with the SRV processor/adapter layers.

**HTTP / synchronous request path.** A single Drupal front controller bootstraps all web requests
[entrypoints.md §1]. **~28 modules declare routes** [entrypoints.md §2] over three route families: the
Application/Lead workflow admin UI and CRUD, the per-region payment-gateway callback/webhook endpoints
(ComGate / Netopia / MAIB / Moneta), and a JSON/session API (session token, user-exists,
magic-link, login, activation-mail). These are served by **~56 controllers** and **~185 forms** — a
data-entry-heavy admin platform [entrypoints.md §3, §7]. Some routes are also declared as **REST resources**
(e.g. the Facebook CAPI relay resource) [integrations.md §3].

**Event subscribers (the orchestration seam).** **6 event subscribers**, of which **three** react to the custom
**application-status-change event** (notification, reaction, scoring) — the platform's main orchestration seam
[entrypoints.md §6; SRV0002; UC0002]. This dispatch is **synchronous, in-request, on every Application save**, not
a decoupled bus (HS01) — see §8.

**Entity-save side-effects.** A defining operational trait: much "orchestration" is implemented as **side-effects
of entity saves** rather than explicit jobs — the Application status fan-out (HS01), and the Transaction PAID
"money hub" that recomputes the campaign, splits overpayment, promotes recurring/voucher, grants roles and sends
mail, all inside the save from every write path (HS03) [DOMAIN-kernel §4; CONSISTENCY-boundaries §2–§3;
FLW0002/0003]. This couples cross-aggregate writes into one request thread with no transaction wrapping.

**Scheduled (cron) processors.** **Cron-driven work in 9 modules** [entrypoints.md §8]: recurring-payment charges per
gateway (RecurringPayment-Processor, UC0007), bank reconciliation (Reconciliation-Processor: Moneta poll + IMAP
import, UC0008), campaign deadline lifecycle (campaign uncomplete, UC0011), voucher, plus a scheduled-publish cron
service (ScheduledPublish-Processor, UC0022) [SRV-target-list Layer P]. A separate cron-invoked **CSV export**
writes reporting files to `/tmp` (CSV-Export-Processor, UC0017) [integrations.md §8; HS07].

**Queue workers.** **5 queue workers** [entrypoints.md §8]: email dispatch, Mautic CRM sync, Elasticsearch
indexing (SearchIndex-Processor, UC0018), and two campaign-recommendation workers (scoring + ML training). The
search-indexing queue and the Mautic CRM-sync queue are the genuinely-eventual paths; the email queue worker is **dead code** (mailing runs
synchronously with the queue disabled — HS13), and the recommendation queue is fed by a **commented-out /
dormant** event (HS14; INV27) [CONSISTENCY-boundaries §2; SRV-target-list].

**CLI / console commands.** Drush + Drupal Console commands for OneDrive invoice import (UC0019), ComGate
transfer sync, Elasticsearch bulk upload, and a set of **one-time data-migration commands** (backfills, not
runtime) [entrypoints.md §8].

**Environment / tenant gating.** Gating is by **runtime country branches and hardcoded country checks** rather
than by configuration: recurring cron and bank imports are CZ-gated by hardcoded checks, and the RO Netopia cron
notably **lacks an environment guard** — an operational risk (HS11) [DOMAIN-kernel §4]. Secrets are read from
settings/env for most integrations, but hardcoded credentials exist in two source locations (OneDrive, Facebook
CAPI) — flagged for target-state hardening, not a current-behaviour defect claim [integrations.md §11].

## 8. Architectural Risks

The top 5 structural risks, derived from DOMAIN hotspots (HSxx), CONSISTENCY-boundaries and the coverage gaps.
(The full 15-hotspot map is in `DOMAIN-kernel §4`; this section prioritises the five with the widest structural
blast radius for a rewrite.)

**Risk 1 — No real consistency boundaries: orchestration and money logic are non-transactional entity-save
side-effects.**
*Evidence:* HS01 (status orchestration as a save side-effect — every Application save unconditionally dispatches
the status event to 3 synchronous subscribers, no changed-state guard, no idempotence, no isolation, downstream
raw-SQL writes), HS03 (the Transaction PAID save runs campaign recompute + overpayment split + recurring/voucher
promotion + role grant + email/Slack + a nested cascade save, from every write path, re-entrant, no callback
idempotence); CONSISTENCY-boundaries "headline truth" (no DB FKs; critical cross-aggregate flows execute
synchronously and non-transactionally); INV01/03/07/21; FLW0001/0002/0003.
*Impact:* A mid-flight failure leaves a partially-updated cross-aggregate graph; re-saves re-log/re-send; nested
saves risk recursion/extra rows. This is the deepest structural issue and the reason a rewrite must lift these
into first-class transactional commands + idempotent, decoupled events.

**Risk 2 — Overloaded universal party/Contact + Application/Profile god-objects, with no RI or uniqueness.**
*Evidence:* HS08 (one Contact represents child/fundraiser/patron/school/employer/lead by discriminator, soft
references only, no unique key on national-number/phone/email, classification written by a raw email-keyed update
with **no LIMIT** that can overwrite unrelated Contacts), HS10 (Application ties ~20 soft references and
ApplicationProfile holds ~100 fields ×2 per Application, with cross-profile owner-coupling and raw-SQL writes
bypassing the entity layer); INV15/23; db-inventory §2, §4.
*Impact:* Duplicates accumulate (forcing the destructive manual merge in Risk 3), classification writes can hit
unintended records, and the god-objects resist safe change. A rewrite must split party roles and add real
identity/uniqueness constraints.

**Risk 3 — Destructive, non-transactional dedup/merge and anti-erasure GDPR.**
*Evidence:* HS09 (Contact dedup + lead pairing + Organisation dedup all run with no DB transaction, no dry-run,
hard-delete duplicate Contacts *and* their owning Users bypassing cancel/anonymise, reparent only some references
leaving others dangling, no country/role scoping), HS06 (GDPR erasure only blanks-in-place and re-upserts the
user to Mautic which re-creates the contact with names intact — right-to-erasure not satisfied); INV17/18/19;
FLW0020/0023/0024/0025.
*Impact:* Irreversible data loss / half-merged graphs on failure, dangling references, and a GDPR-compliance gap.

**Risk 4 — Implicit CZ/RO/MD multi-tenancy with cross-tenant data leakage and vendor-lock adapters.**
*Evidence:* HS11 (country behaviour is runtime branches + per-country hardcoding, no tenant/country column;
per-gateway payment split with Netopia the strongest vendor lock-in via a vendored SDK; RO tax-redirect is a
distinct flow; RO Netopia cron lacks an environment guard), HS07 (CSV export dumps national numbers/names/
addresses/risk verdicts to `/tmp` with **no country/tenant filter** — exports are global across CZ/RO/MD, files
never deleted, unencrypted PII at rest); SRV-architecture-map §4 (vendor lock-in table); integrations.md §10.
*Impact:* Cross-tenant PII exposure, no clean per-country isolation, and hard-to-swap payment/CRM/storage vendors.

**Risk 5 — Dormant/planned and thin/un-mined subsystems present in the codebase but not live behaviour.**
*Evidence:* HS14 (the campaign-recommendation subsystem — UC0021, AG7 Account — is inert on 5 independent grounds:
event dispatch commented out, module not installed, classifier YAML commented, ML library absent, storage field
commented out; INV27), HS16 (thin/partial-coverage SRVs whose flows have now been mined in batch 4 — search-index
sync (C10) → FLW0032, scheduled publish + Workflow-Engine (C11) → FLW0033, the ops-alert listener → FLW0034 —
which **confirmed the flows but surfaced residual current-state gaps rather than closing them** (drain scheduling
unenforced, transition-legality unenforced, ES audit sub-flow not indexed), plus the **confirmed not-implemented**
Facebook inbound lead webhook); UC-srv-traceability §4 (post-batch-4, the only non-Confirmed coverage is the
dormant recommendation processor UC0021 and the not-implemented FB inbound webhook UC0013).
*Impact:* A rewrite must **not** treat dormant/stub features as live current-state behaviour; each requires an
explicit drop-or-rebuild decision. The batch-4 flows (FLW0032/FLW0033/FLW0034) are now mined, but their residual
current-state gaps (drain scheduling, transition-legality, ES audit indexing) must be resolved before relying on
SRV coverage as complete, correct behaviour.
