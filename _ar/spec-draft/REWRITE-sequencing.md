## 1. Sequencing principles

Three ordering forces, applied in this priority:

1. **Domain core before integration boundaries before infrastructure/ops.** Build the invariants that own money,
   party identity and case state first; wrap external vendors behind ports next; leave search/index, ops-alerting,
   scheduled-publish and dormant subsystems last (they are thin, carry residual current-state gaps after batch-4 mining, or are inert — R19/R20; HS16; SRV-target-list
   Layers D → O → A → P).
2. **Stabilise the highest-risk, highest-blast-radius blockers first.** The decision pack's Rewrite Blockers
   (B1–B8) gate everything built on top of them: nothing that reads status is trustworthy before B1; nothing that
   moves money is safe before B2; every merge is destructive before B3/B4. Blockers are scheduled ahead of pure
   architectural risks.
3. **Follow the dependency direction, not the org chart.** The allowed dependency direction (Processors →
   Orchestrators/Domain; Domain → Adapters via ports; SRV-architecture-map §3) dictates that the state engine (B1)
   and the Party model (B3) are prerequisites for almost everything else, so they lead; adapters and processors
   trail the domain services they serve.

> **Why domain-core-first is correct for Patronus specifically.** The deepest current-state defect is that
> "orchestration" and "money logic" are non-transactional entity-save side-effects with no consistency boundaries
> (ARCH0001 §8 Risk 1; CONSISTENCY headline truth; HS01/HS03). You cannot safely isolate vendors or add processors
> on top of a core that has no enforced state machine, no atomic money command and no party identity — so the core
> must be stabilised before the boundaries that call into it.

## 2. Recommended rewrite order (phases)

Six phases, domain-core-first. Each phase lists the blockers/ADRs it lands, the target SRV layer it touches, and
the aggregates/contexts in scope. Phases 0–3 are the **stabilisation spine** (see §4); phases 4–5 are boundaries
and infrastructure.

### Phase 0 — Foundations: tenant dimension + party identity (the model everything else needs)
- **Lands:** B3 (partial — identity), B6 (partial — tenant dimension); ADR-05, ADR-06, ADR-10.
- **Layer / scope:** Domain (D) — Party-&-Contact-Management (SRV0012), Identity-&-Access (SRV0015),
  Application-Lifecycle (SRV0001); contexts C7/C9/C1; AG7 (EN0008/EN0006), AG1 (EN0001/EN0002).
- **Why first.** No FKs, no identity uniqueness and no tenant column are the root structural facts
  (ARCH0001 §6; HS08/HS11; INV23). A canonical Party model with real uniqueness and an explicit CZ/RO/MD tenant
  dimension are prerequisites for safe merges (Phase 1), safe money attribution (Phase 2) and scoped exports
  (Phase 3). Decomposing the Application/Profile god-object belongs here because scoring/status/money all read it.

### Phase 1 — Case-state engine + safe party lifecycle
- **Lands:** B1, B4; ADR-01, ADR-07.
- **Layer / scope:** Orchestrator (O) + Domain (D) — Application-Status-Orchestrator (SRV0002), Workflow-Engine,
  Party-&-Contact-Management; context C1/C7; AG1/AG7/AG8.
- **Why here.** With identity in place (Phase 0), lift the ~66-state workflow into an authoritative, enforced,
  idempotent state machine (B1/ADR-01) and replace the destructive, non-transactional dedup/merge with
  transactional, preview-able, archive-not-delete merges (B4/ADR-07). Both depend on Phase 0's identity/tenant
  model; both are prerequisites for status-driven money and scoring flows in Phase 2.

### Phase 2 — Money integrity: atomic payment core + recurring + reconciliation
- **Lands:** B2, B7; ADR-02, ADR-03, ADR-04 (domain side), ADR-12 (reconciliation legs).
- **Layer / scope:** Domain (D) + Processor (P) — Payment-Processing (SRV0007),
  Campaign-&-Story-Lifecycle (SRV0005), Bank-Reconciliation (SRV0009), RecurringPayment-Processor,
  Reconciliation-Processor; contexts C4/C3/C5; AG3/AG2/AG4/AG6/AG12/AG13.
- **Why here.** This is the second stabilisation pillar. Make the PAID transition an atomic command with idempotent
  decoupled events (B2/ADR-02), charge recurring under a claim/lock with confirmed-settlement PAID (ADR-03), and
  make each reconciliation leg retryable/idempotent (B7/ADR-12). It depends on Phase 1's state engine (completion
  cascades App↔Campaign) and Phase 0's tenant dimension (per-country gateway/bank routing). Callback
  authentication (ADR-04) starts here on the domain side and completes with the adapters in Phase 4.

### Phase 3 — Compliance surface: scoring, GDPR erasure, tenant-scoped reporting
- **Lands:** B5, B6 (complete — export scoping); ADR-08, ADR-09; scoring-write correctness (part of ADR-05/ADR-02).
- **Layer / scope:** Domain (D) + Processor (P) — Scoring-&-Risk (SRV0003), Identity-&-Access (SRV0015),
  Reporting-ReadModel (SRV0010), CSV-Export-Processor; contexts C2/C9/C5; AG9/AG7; read-models EN0031/EN0032.
- **Why here.** Right-to-erasure (B5/ADR-08) and cross-tenant PII export scoping (B6/ADR-09) are legal/data-
  protection obligations; they depend on Phase 0's Party model + tenant dimension and Phase 1's safe party
  lifecycle. Scoring's three-aggregate write is made transactional/idempotent here on top of the Phase 1 engine and
  Phase 0 identity (no more no-LIMIT email-keyed classification).

### Phase 4 — Integration boundaries: adapters behind ports
- **Lands:** R14, R6/R4 (callback-auth adapter side); ADR-11, ADR-04 (adapter completion).
- **Layer / scope:** Adapter (A) — the 16 target adapters: ComGate/Netopia/MAIB (C4), Moneta-AISP/BankMail-IMAP/
  ComGate-TransferSync (C5), MVČR/ARES (C2), Mautic/FacebookCAPI/Analytics/Email/WhoisXML (C8), OneDrive (C6),
  Elasticsearch (C10), Slack+Telegram (C11); + Document-Generation-&-Fulfilment (C6) and
  Transactional-Messaging-Orchestrator (C8).
- **Why after the core.** Adapters serve the stabilised domain services (dependency direction, SRV §3). Isolate each
  external boundary behind a port (ADR-11), completing payment callback authentication (ADR-04) and asynchronous
  messaging (ADR-08 sibling) against the atomic money core built in Phase 2. Netopia's vendored SDK (strongest
  lock-in) and MAIB's mutual-TLS are wrapped here, not earlier.

### Phase 5 — Infrastructure, async processors & dormant/thin subsystems (last)
- **Lands:** R16 (App↔Campaign reconcile), R18 (messaging decoupling completion), R19, R20, B8; ADR-12 (drop-or-
  rebuild + mine).
- **Layer / scope:** Processor (P) + Orchestrator/Adapter (O/A) — SearchIndex-Processor + Elasticsearch-Adapter
  (C10), ScheduledPublish-Processor + Workflow-Engine (C11), Ops-Logging-Adapters (C11),
  CampaignRecommendation-Processor (C3, dormant); contexts C10/C11/C3.
- **Why last.** These are thin, now mined but carrying residual current-state gaps (FLW0032/33/34, was FL055/FL057/FL059) or inert (HS14/HS16; INV27; UC-srv-traceability §4).
  Each needs an explicit **drop-or-rebuild** decision (B8/ADR-12) and, where kept, its residual current-state gaps must be resolved before it
  can be relied upon. The App↔Campaign reconcile-not-alert fix (R16) completes here as a background reconciliation
  once the core aggregates are authoritative.

**Layer order across phases:** Domain/Orchestrator (Phases 0–3) → Adapters (Phase 4) → Processors/Infra
(Phase 5), matching the D → O → A → P target taxonomy and the allowed dependency direction (SRV-architecture-map
§2/§3).

## 4. Stabilisation strategy (what to lock down first)

The three pillars to lock down before broad feature reconstruction, in order. These correspond to the phases above
and to the decision pack's blockers.

### Pillar A — Party model (lock first) — Phase 0
Add real identity/uniqueness and an explicit tenant dimension, and split the overloaded Contact into party roles.
This is first because it is the **root cause** node: duplicates (HS08/INV23) force the destructive merges (HS09),
the no-tenant-column fact (HS11) drives the cross-tenant export leak (HS07), and every soft reference points at the
same overloaded store. Locking the Party model down removes the structural reason the other defects exist.
*Grounds: R07/R08/R13; HS08/HS10/HS11; INV15/23; ADR-05/06/10; B3/B6.*

### Pillar B — Status engine (lock second) — Phase 1
Lift status orchestration into an authoritative, enforced, idempotent state machine and make party merges
transactional. This is second because status is the spine every process area rides on (ŽÁDOST FRONT/BACK, RISK,
CONTENT all key off the ~66-state field), and because the money and scoring flows in Pillar C cascade **through**
status (completion → complete; scoring → `scoring_ok`; contract signing → `contract_signed`). Nothing that reads
or writes status is trustworthy until the engine is authoritative.
*Grounds: R01/R02/R09/R10; HS01/HS02/HS09; INV04/17/18/19; ADR-01/07; B1/B4.*

### Pillar C — Money integrity (lock third) — Phase 2
Make the PAID transition atomic, decouple its side-effects into idempotent events, charge recurring under a
claim/lock with confirmed-settlement PAID, enforce a DB-unique payment identity + idempotency key, and make the
reconciliation legs retryable/idempotent. This is third because it depends on both prior pillars (money attributes
to a Party, completion cascades through the state engine) but is the highest-consequence correctness domain — it
protects donor money and finance reconciliation.
*Grounds: R03/R04/R05/R06/R15; HS03/HS04/HS05/HS12; INV01/03/07/08/09/21/25; ADR-02/03/04/12; B2/B7.*

> **Compliance (Phase 3) rides immediately on the three pillars:** right-to-erasure and tenant-scoped exports are
> legal obligations, but they are *enabled* by Pillars A (party/tenant) and B (safe lifecycle), so they are locked
> down right after the pillars, before the vendor boundaries (Phase 4) and infra (Phase 5).

## 5. Sequencing rationale (highest-risk flows + highest business-impact areas)

Why this order, tied to the highest-risk flows (by UC/FLW) and the highest-business-impact process areas.

**Highest-risk flows drive Pillars B and C.** The mined-flow evidence concentrates risk in exactly the flows the
early phases stabilise:
- **Status fan-out** (UC0002; FLW0001/0002) — non-idempotent, non-transactional, any-state-reachable — is the
  single deepest structural issue (ARCH0001 §8 Risk 1; HS01/HS02) → **Pillar B / Phase 1**.
- **The PAID money hub** (UC0006; FLW0003/0006) and **recurring charge** (UC0007; FLW0007) — re-entrant saves,
  optimistic PAID, no idempotency key (HS03/HS04/HS12; INV25) → **Pillar C / Phase 2**.
- **Dedup/merge** (UC0016; FLW0023/0024/0025) — irreversible, non-transactional, hard-deletes Users (HS09;
  INV17/18/19) → **Pillar B / Phase 1**, resting on the identity from Pillar A.
- **Bank reconciliation** (UC0008; FLW0011/0012/0013) — silent skipped-day, row-capped match, brittle parse
  (HS05) → **Pillar C / Phase 2**.
- **GDPR erasure** (UC0014/UC0015; FLW0020) — anti-erasure re-upsert (HS06) and **CSV export** (UC0017; FLW0027)
  — cross-tenant PII (HS07) → **Phase 3**, once the party/tenant model exists.

**Highest-business-impact areas map onto the same order.** Across the seven current-state process areas (ŽÁDOST
FRONT/BACK, RISK, DONATIONS, FINANCE, CONTENT, AFFIL):
- **DONATIONS + FINANCE** carry donor money and reconciliation — the highest business consequence — and are
  stabilised in Phase 2 (Pillar C), immediately after their prerequisites.
- **ŽÁDOST FRONT/BACK** (the case lifecycle) and **RISK** (scoring) ride the status engine and party identity —
  Phases 0–1 (Pillars A/B) — and RISK's compliance-sensitive classification write is finished in Phase 3.
- **CONTENT** (campaign/story publish, feedback, blog) depends on the state engine and campaign completion, so it
  reconstructs on top of Phases 1–2; the App↔Campaign reconcile-not-alert fix (INV04/R16) completes in Phase 5.
- **AFFIL / marketing** (Mautic, Facebook, analytics) is an integration-boundary concern (Phase 4) and its inbound
  FB Lead webhook is a not-implemented stub (Phase 5 drop-or-rebuild) — correctly last, as it is outbound-signal
  or inert (HS16; ES0007).

**Why boundaries and infra come last.** Vendor adapters (Phase 4) only make sense once the domain services they
serve are stable and expose ports (SRV §3/§4); the strongest lock-ins (Netopia vendored SDK, MAIB mutual-TLS) are
wrapped against an already-atomic money core. Infra/dormant subsystems (Phase 5) are thin, now mined with residual gaps, or inert
(R19/R20; HS14/HS16; INV27) — building them early would encode unknown or dead behaviour as live, which the
evidence explicitly warns against (ARCH0001 §8 Risk 5).

**One-line order.** Party model → State engine → Money integrity → Compliance surface → Vendor boundaries →
Infra/dormant. (Pillars A→B→C are the non-negotiable spine; everything else trails their dependency edges.)
